Chapter 12: Stoichiometry


    Chemical and Mathematical Equations

    1. Equations

  • A balanced chemical equation provides the same kind of quantitative information that a recipe does.

  • Chemists use balanced chemical equations as a basis to calculate how much reactant is needed or product is formed in a reaction.

  • A balanced chemical equation can be interpreted in terms of different quantities, including numbers of atoms, molecules, or moles; mass; and volume.

  • Mass and atoms are conserved in every chemical reaction.


      1. Chemical equations show how many reactants will yield a certain amount of products

      2. Stoichiometry: Study of how quantities are calculated in chemical reactions

      3. Balanced chemical equations have different meanings depending on what scale they are viewed


    2. Various Quantities

    Atoms

    • Balanced equations show the number and type of each atom in the reactants and products

    • Neither of those change throughout the reaction

      • Molecules

    • Balanced equations show the ratio of atomic moles that combine to form a certain molecule

    • Increasing the ratio yields more molecules (1:3:2 = 1 molecule || 10:30:20 = 10 molecules)

      Moles

    • Balanced equations show how many moles of reactants and products there are

    • Indicated by the coefficients

    • Often not the same throughout the reaction

    • Helps you figure out how many moles of products or reactants there will be

      Mass

    • Law of Conservation of Mass

    • Reactants and products cumulatively have the same mass

      Volume

    • Number of moles shows the volume of STP gases

    • Often not the same throughout the reaction

Go look at some example problems for more information c. Percent Yield and Limiting Reagent 1. Excess and Limiting Reagents * In a chemical reaction, an insufficient quantity of any of the reactants will limit the amount of product that forms. i. Unbalanced equations may contain more or less reactants than necessary ii. Reagent: Substance used in a chemical reaction iii. Limiting Reagent: Substance that determines the minimum amount of product that can be produced in a reaction iv. Can only be found by converting the problem’s units into moles v. Excess Reagent: Surplus substance that does not undergo reaction 2. Percent Yield * The percent yield is a measure of the efficiency of a reaction carried out in the laboratory. i. Nothing is really 100% certain in life ii. Theoretical Yield: Maximum amount of products that could be formed by the reactants iii. Actual Yield: Amount of products formed by reactants in an experiment iv. Percent Yield: Ratio of the theoretical yield to the actual yield, expressed as a percentage v. Similar to the accepted and experimental values in error calculations vi. Percentage yields should not be higher than 100% vii. Percent Yield = (Actual Yield / Theoretical Yield) X 100 3. Reasons for Low Percent Yields i. Partially-completed reactions ii. Impure reactants iii. Loss of product iv. Unintended side reactions v. Unwanted products vi. Inaccurate measurements
